Kenya triples nuclear target, Egypt plans expansion, US-Africa summit delivers

A big week for African nuclear energy. Kenya's president announced a 3,000 MW nuclear commitment by 2034. Egypt revealed plans to expand beyond its current four-unit build. And the first US-Africa Nuclear Energy Summit concluded in Accra with concrete cooperation agreements. Here's what happened, with sources.

Kenya: 3,000 MW nuclear by 2034

President William Ruto opened the International Conference on Nuclear Energy 2026 in Nairobi (Aug 19) with a major announcement: Kenya plans to commence construction of a 2,000 MW nuclear power plant in Siaya County in 2027, with commissioning expected by 2034. The target includes 3,000 MW of nuclear capacity as part of a broader 10,000 MW expansion from the current 3,300 MW installed capacity.

"We have recalibrated our long-term growth trajectory from 1,500MW to a 5,500MW renewable energy development pipeline," said Peter Njenga, CEO of KenGen. The plan includes 2,000 MW nuclear alongside 700 MW hydro and new geothermal projects.

Source: President of Kenya — Statement at ICE 2026 (PDF) · ABC News (Aug 19)

Egypt: El Dabaa expansion planned

Egypt plans to build two additional nuclear power plants beyond the four-unit El Dabaa facility currently under construction with Russia's Rosatom. The expansion was reported by NEI Magazine (Aug 20) following statements from Egyptian officials. Unit 1's containment structure recently completed its fourth tier, with first concrete for Unit 2 poured in June 2026.

Source: NEI Magazine (Aug 20) · World Nuclear News (Aug 20)

US-Africa Nuclear Energy Summit (Accra, Aug 19–21)

The first US-Africa Nuclear Energy Summit (USANES 2026) concluded in Accra, Ghana with representatives from 54 African nations. Key outcomes:

  • Regional Energy Training Centre established in Ghana for West African nuclear professionals
  • US committed to partnering with Ghana on nuclear power deployment
  • Ghana, Kenya, Uganda, Nigeria, and Ethiopia signed nuclear cooperation agreements with US entities
  • Discussions on SMRs, microreactors, and nuclear desalination

Source: Brecorder (Aug 22) · GBC Ghana (Aug 21) · NewsWire (Aug 22)

US-Namibia nuclear cooperation

The US expressed interest in partnering with Namibia on nuclear energy, including SMRs and microreactors for mining operations. This follows Namibia's announcement of plans for a nuclear power plant and uranium processing facility.

Source: allAfrica (Aug 18)

Botswana: nuclear science vision

Minister of Communications and Knowledge Services Dean Matholeng Molefe outlined Botswana's vision for peaceful nuclear applications, noting the country's uranium reserves and plans for a nuclear power plant.

Source: CAJ News Africa (Aug 20)
