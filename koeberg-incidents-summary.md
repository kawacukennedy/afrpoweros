# Koeberg Radiation Incidents (June–July 2026) — Cited, Confidence-Labelled Summary

**Prepared for:** Winile Ximba
**Prepared by:** Kennedy Kawacu, dataset builder, AfrPowerOS
**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Method:** Verified claims drawn from primary/regulator-adjacent statements (National
Nuclear Regulator, Eskom official statements) and reputable reporting (AP, TimesLIVE,
Daily Maverick, Nuclear Engineering International, IOL, SAnews). Every claim is tagged:
**Verified** (multi-source / official statements), **Reported** (single reputable outlet or

## TL;DR

During a scheduled outage of Koeberg Unit 2 (outage since **April 2026**), work on the
steam generators produced brief, localised spikes in airborne radioactive contamination
inside the containment building. **No radioactive material was released to the
environment**, there was **no risk to the public**, all events were classified **INES
Level 0 ("No Safety Significance")** — the lowest possible rating — and the highest
individual dose recorded was far below a dental X-ray exposure. The regulator (NNR) said
the events "did not meet the criteria for classification as a nuclear or radiological
incident or emergency."

Importantly, although media first reported **three** incidents (30 Jun, 2 Jul, 7 Jul),
Eskom later disclosed a **fourth** (16 Jul) — disclosed "for the sake of transparency"
after days of public speculation.

## Timeline of Events

### Event 1 — 30 June 2026
- **What happened:** A worker's electronic personal radiation dosimeter alarmed during
  steam-generator inspection work. **Verified**
- Eskom said it did **not** classify this as an airborne-contamination event; investigations
  found no significant problem and workers resumed after safety surveys. **Reported**
  (TimesLIVE, citing Eskom chief nuclear officer Velaphi Ntuli)
- **Confidence:** Verified (occurrence + no classification); Reported (details).

### Event 2 — 2 July 2026
- **What happened:** An area radiation monitor detected a rising airborne-radioactivity
  trend inside the Unit 2 containment building. A precautionary evacuation followed; an
  alarm confirmed airborne contamination as workers left. **Verified**
- **Workers involved:** TimesLIVE reports 31 workers evacuated; Daily Maverick reports 51
  evacuated, and ~245 workers who had been in the building that day underwent whole-body
  counting (a test for internal contamination). **Reported — numbers differ across outlets,
  not yet reconciled.**
- **Cause:** A portable ventilation unit failed — TimesLIVE attributes it to ducting bent
  out of position; Daily Maverick quotes an electrical cord that burnt out. **Reported —
  cause details vary; NNR investigation ongoing.**
- **Confidence:** Verified (occurrence, evacuation, airborne contamination); Reported
  (worker counts and cause details).

### Event 3 — 7 July 2026
- **What happened:** Radiation-protection staff detected airborne contamination leaking
  from the sealed work "tent" (a temporary enclosure around the work zone) into the wider
  containment building during eddy-current testing. Workers were evacuated pre-emptively,
  before alarms escalated. **Verified**
- 14 workers underwent whole-body monitoring; a further 7 voluntarily requested scans.
  **Reported**
- **Cause:** A portable ventilation unit apparently lost power after becoming disconnected
  from its power supply. **Reported**
- **Confidence:** Verified (occurrence, leak from tent, evacuation); Reported (worker
  counts, cause).

### Event 4 — 16 July 2026 (disclosed by Eskom on 19 July)
- **What happened:** Monitoring detected a further increase in airborne contamination during
  steam-generator testing. Work was stopped; no alarm was triggered; the area was cleaned.
  **Verified**
- Eskom disclosed this event publicly only in its **19 July** media briefing, after the
  initial three incidents had been reported on 16 July. **Verified**
- NNR's (and initial media's) first statements referenced **three** events; the fourth came
  from Eskom's subsequent statement. **Verified**
- **Confidence:** Verified (occurrence and disclosure timeline, per Eskom statement).

## Key Facts (across all events)

- **No environmental release:** Eskom and the NNR both state that "at no stage was there any
  release of radioactive material beyond the containment structures" and there was no impact
  on surrounding communities or the environment. **Verified**
- **No public risk / not a nuclear emergency:** NNR spokesperson Gino Moomsamy: "There was no
  safety impact on the public. It is an internal operational issue" and the events were not
  categorised as a nuclear emergency. **Verified**
- **INES Level 0:** all events classified Level 0 (No Safety Significance) — the lowest INES
  rating. **Verified**
- **Highest recorded dose:** ~20 microsieverts, cited by Eskom against Koeberg's annual
  occupational limit of 20,000 microsieverts (i.e., ~0.1% of the annual limit). **Reported**
  (Daily Maverick). NNR separately said screened workers recorded contamination below the
  level of a dental X-ray (~1-10 µSv). **Verified** (NNR statement via AP)
- **No iodine tablets issued:** reactor fuel had been removed from containment before
  maintenance and stored in the fuel building, so no radioactive iodine release was
  possible. **Verified**
- **Cause:** Scheduled **Eddy Current Testing (ECT)** of steam generators dislodged small
  quantities of radioactive corrosion products (including cobalt-58), which became airborne
  in the immediate work area. Portable ventilation units suffered power-supply failures on
  at least two occasions. **Verified** (process); **Reported** (cobalt-58 detail, Daily
  Maverick)
- **Regulator status:** The NNR said its "regulatory investigation and verification process
  are active and currently ongoing" (as of 19 July). **Verified**
- **Plant status:** Unit 1 continued operating normally; Unit 2 outage remained on schedule
  for completion **November 2026**. **Verified**
- **Context:** Koeberg (≈40 km north of Cape Town) is Africa's only commercial nuclear power
  plant; two reactors generating ~5% of South Africa's electricity. Its operating licence was
  extended to **2045** (NNR approval Nov 2025). **Verified**

## Confidence-Labelling Key (AfrPowerOS standard)

- **Verified** — confirmed by primary sources (NNR/Eskom statements) and/or consistent across
  multiple reputable outlets.
- **Reported** — stated by a reputable single source (or a detail where outlets differ);
  should be attributed as "reported" in any story.
- **Unverified** — claimed but not yet confirmed on record. None of the core facts in this
  summary are unverified; the worker-count and failure-mode details (Events 1–3) are where
  sources still differ. The NNR investigation was still ongoing as of the date of this summary.
